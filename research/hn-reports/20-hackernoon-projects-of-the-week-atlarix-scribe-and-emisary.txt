Atlarix sets itself apart in a major way: by having a memory that lasts.\n\n\n\n\nAs Amariah Kamau describes it, other AI tools have incredibly short memories. They have to remember everything each time you ask a question. This is not only slow and frustrating, but is also a big waste of energy and resources. You don’t have that problem with Atlarix, as users can create what’s called a “visual blueprint.” The AI can then use this visual blueprint as a guide to keep its memory fresh.\n\n\n\n\nAtlarix is an AI coding copilot that uses visual architecture blueprints as persistent memory, so it doesn’t need to rescan your whole codebase every prompt. Define your APIs, services, agents, and webhooks once, and the AI stays context-aware across sessions\n\n- Amariah Kamau\n\n\n\n\nProof of Usefulness: +80/100\n\nSee Atlarix’s full Proof of Usefulness Report\n\nRead their story on HackerNoon\n\n\n\n\nScribe\nHACKERNOON\nScribe Earns a 39 Proof of Usefulness Score by Building an Agent that Synthesizes Group Chat Links into High-Signal Digests | HackerNoon\nScribe scores 39 on Proof of Usefulness, using AI to track group chats, capture shared links, and deliver curated digests.\n\nIf you’ve ever been involved in a highly active group chat (whether a professional one or one just with friends), you know how easy it is to miss things. Especially when links are being sent out left and right. That’s where Scribe comes in. Scribe is a tool created specifically so you never miss anything posted in your group chat again.\n\n\n\n\nIt constantly checks the group chat for links, and when it finds one, it synthesizes it into a digest. Right now, it’s available only on Telegram, but its website suggests that it will be available on Slack, Discord, and WhatsApp in the future.\n\n\n\n\nNow’s a good time for Scribe to exist because our primary mode of knowledge sharing has shifted to fast-paced group chats, making it critical to have tools that reliably capture and preserve valuable insights before they get lost in the feed.\n\n- Justin Irabor\n\n\n\n\nProof of Usefulness: +39/100\n\nSee Scribe’s full Proof of Usefulness Report\n\nRead their \nHACKERNOON\nScribe Earns a 39 Proof of Usefulness Score by Building an Agent that Synthesizes Group Chat Links into High-Signal Digests | HackerNoon\nScribe scores 39 on Proof of Usefulness, using AI to track group chats, capture shared links, and deliver curated digests.\n on HackerNoon\n\n\n\n\nEmisary\nHACKERNOON\nEmisary Earns a 32 Proof of Usefulness Score by Building an Actionable AI Feedback Diary | HackerNoon\nEmisary scores 32 on Proof of Usefulness, using AI to analyze journal entries and provide behavioral feedback for personal growth.\n\nEverything evolves, so why shouldn’t diaries? Even diaries can use improvement, and that’s what Emisary is setting out to do. Emisary is a personal diary that gives you AI feedback about how you are potentially being perceived by the people around you. All of us, at one point or another, have thought about how others must view us. Using Emisary, we can get an estimate.\n\n\n\n\nBut how does Emisary figure all this out?
